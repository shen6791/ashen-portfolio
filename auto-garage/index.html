About the content: this is a ready-to-brand template. The business name, phone, email, address and hours are placeholders, the photos and hero video are free stock media, and there are no made-up reviews or statistics. Where a detail is missing the page says so instead of inventing one. The booking system and WhatsApp integration are working code.

What it does

The brief was a site that looks like a premium automotive brand, loads quickly on a phone, and turns visitors into booking requests.

Booking requests that arrive

Name, phone, vehicle, service and date are validated in the browser and again on the server, then sent by email (Resend) or to a webhook.

Honest success and error states

"Request sent" only appears if a delivery channel accepted the message. With nothing configured the form says setup is required.

Spam protection

Hidden honeypot field, minimum fill time, same-origin check, rate limiting, and optional Cloudflare Turnstile.

WhatsApp and calling

A floating WhatsApp button with a pre-filled enquiry, and tap-to-call links on mobile. Both use the owner's own numbers from settings.

Editable by the owner

Services, hours, photos, gallery, statistics and reviews all live in one config file. Contact details come from environment variables.

SEO and accessibility

Open Graph image, sitemap, robots.txt, structured data that only uses confirmed details, keyboard navigation, focus states and reduced-motion support.

Services built around the vehicle

Eight service cards with clear descriptions, icons and hover feedback, staggered in as the page scrolls.

  • Diagnostics, servicing, brakes, oil, A/C, suspension, electrical and pre-purchase checks
  • Services are one editable list and feed the booking form's dropdown

A booking form people finish

Short, labelled fields with inline errors that move focus to the first problem, and a loading state on the button while it sends.

  • Phone, date and length checks run on the client and again on the server
  • Registration number and message are optional
  • The form is clearly a request, not a confirmed appointment

A workshop gallery with a lightbox

A responsive image grid with hover effects. Click a photo for a full-size view that works with arrow keys and Escape.

  • Images are served as AVIF or WebP and lazy loaded
  • The owner swaps in real workshop photos by editing one list

A few decisions worth mentioning

  • Never a fake success. The API only returns success when email or the webhook really accepted the request. Network failures, missing setup and delivery errors each show their own message.
  • No invented business claims. Reviews, statistics, certifications and contact details are left empty or marked as placeholders until the owner supplies real ones. The structured data skips anything not configured.
  • Bots get nothing. A filled honeypot or an instant submit receives a silent fake success and delivers nothing, so bots get no signal to adapt to.
  • Motion with restraint. Entrance and scroll animations use Motion, the hero parallax and background video switch off for reduced-motion and data-saver users, and there is no loading screen.
  • Secrets stay on the server. API keys live in environment variables and the form posts to a server route, so nothing sensitive reaches the browser.
